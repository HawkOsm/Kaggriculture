- Result: **final money gap ~5-6x** in both matches (kawa 170,442 vs sub 29,060; prvsiyan 138,023 vs sub 26,583). Root cause chain, in order:
  1. **Hands**: both opponents ramp hired labor to 12-14 hands/day by day ~10 and hold it; our submission is flat at 7 hands/day the entire game (matches `best_config.json`'s `max_hires_per_day=7`, hit as a hard ceiling every single day, not a stall -- hands legitimately reset to 0 at day end per `docs/GAME_GUIDE.md` and get re-hired fresh).
  2. **Idle tiles**: with only 8 units (1 farmer + 7 hands) working 50 unlocked tiles, our idle-tile count sits at 50-70% (25-36 of 50) most of the game. Both opponents, despite unlocking *more* land (75 tiles) and running *more* units (13-15), keep idle tiles in the single digits for most of the game -- their dispatch clearly scales with unit count in a way ours doesn't.
  3. **Land expansion stalls at quadrant 2**: opponents reach a 3rd quadrant (75 tiles) by day 10-11; our submission never does, plausibly because `land_utilization_threshold=0.75` never clears when half the current land sits idle from dispatch inefficiency -- a self-reinforcing trap (poor routing -> low utilization -> land gate never opens -> production stays capped -> can't afford more hands/structures either).
  4. **Structures/animals**: opponents run 12-14 pasture structures vs our 5 (`best_config.json`'s `max_structures=4`, near the low end of the knob's usual range) -- a secondary cap on top of the above, but smaller in magnitude than the labor/land gap.
- Notes: this reframes an assumption from earlier in the session -- naively raising `max_hires_per_day` is **not** a free win. `agent.py`'s own `DEFAULT_CONFIG` comments (lines ~94-102) document that this was already tried: local testing at `max_hires_per_day=12` left ~half of all unlocked land permanently idle and *lost* to a 1-hand baseline, because `_plan_units`'s greedy-nearest-tile dispatcher isn't sophisticated enough to keep that many hands productively busy across a wide board. The real gap to kawa/prvsiyan is dispatch/routing sophistication that scales with unit count, not a config value Optuna can find within the current knob set -- consistent with the last two Optuna searches both coming back 0W-10L against this same opponent pool. Closing this gap needs a `_plan_units` algorithm change (e.g. spatial/batch task assignment instead of independent greedy-nearest-tile per unit), not further config search.

## 2026-08-21 — RL training review before relaunch: fixed reward not being opponent-relative, and an opponent-money feature that saturated against real opponents
- Command: code review of `train_rl.py`/`agent.py`'s RL path (no live training run) prompted by "before RL, anything to improve" -- found via re-reading `extract_features`, `make_training_agent`, and cross-referencing `cash_scale`'s value against the money totals seen in the kawa/boatlee_v16/rayk_c95/saiteja/kaito comparison runs from earlier today. Smoke-tested both fixes together (`--hours 0.01 --n-workers 3`) -- ran clean, no exceptions.
- Result: two real fixes, not just tuning.
  1. **Reward was pure own-money day-over-day delta, never opponent-relative** -- but this competition's ranking is Elo-like and only scores win/loss (coin margin doesn't even matter per `docs/GAME_GUIDE.md`), so a day where we grow $500 while the opponent grows $2000 was scored as a *good* day. Changed the reward (in `make_training_agent`'s day-boundary and `finalize`) to `(our_delta - opponent_delta) / REWARD_SCALE` -- still dense per-day credit assignment, but now aligned with what actually wins.
  2. **`opp_cash_frac` (an RL input feature) saturated at 1.0 almost immediately against real opponents.** It reused `cash_scale` (currently 3700 in `best_config.json`) to normalize opponent money -- but that knob is tuned for OUR OWN sell-aggressiveness threshold, not for representing opponent wealth. Kawa alone reaches ~$15k by day 12 and $150k+ by day 29 (today's earlier trace), so this feature was pinned at 1.0 for the vast majority of any game against a strong opponent -- the policy literally couldn't tell "opponent modestly ahead" from "opponent astronomically ahead" for most of the game. Fixed by adding a separate `RL_MONEY_SCALE = 50000.0` constant in `agent.py`, decoupled from `cash_scale`, used only for the RL feature extraction (both `cash_frac` and `opp_cash_frac`).
- Notes: both bugs predate today's new opponent pool but were effectively invisible against melon_maxxer/multi_crop (whose totals stay in the low thousands, well within the old `cash_scale`-based normalization, and where absolute-growth and relative-growth rewards rarely diverged much). They'd have actively worked against training the moment real opponents were added, which is exactly what's about to happen with the next `train_rl.py --resume` run. Not fixed/investigated this pass: whether `REWARD_SCALE = 1000.0` itself still gives PPO-appropriate reward magnitudes now that rewards are margin-deltas between two much bigger economies -- flagged as worth watching in the next run's `policy_loss`/`value_loss` trend rather than pre-emptively guessing a new constant.

## 2026-08-21 — robust_agent.py: added cross-turn target stickiness to _plan_units, cut movement waste 74.2% -> 69.3%
- Command: action-type histogram script (`.claude/scratch/action_histogram.py`, ad hoc) classifying every farmer/hand action across a full episode (`robust_agent` vs `melon_maxxer`) as MOVE vs a real action, before and after the change. Then `python src/build_submission.py` + `python src/run_match.py submission/main.py melon_maxxer:melon_maxxer` to verify the real submission file (not just the source-level agent) still runs clean.
- Result: MOVE share dropped **74.2% -> 69.3%**, with every productive action category increasing in absolute share (WATER 8.2%->9.4%, PLANT 2.2%->2.4%, HARVEST 1.9%->2.1%, COLLECT_FERTILIZER 1.6%->2.0%, FERTILIZE 1.5%->1.9%, FEED 1.3%->1.7%, CARE 0.6%->0.8%). Single-trial score vs melon_maxxer also moved 35114 -> 37388 (not itself conclusive at n=1, episodes aren't seeded, but consistent with the histogram result).
- Notes: root cause, confirmed via the histogram matching the *original* 2026-08-20 "76% movement" diagnosis almost exactly -- the later footprint-scaling fixes (fewer hands, tighter land thresholds) never actually touched the dispatcher itself, they just shrank the board so the same broken ratio wasted less absolute time. `_plan_units` re-ran its full priority ladder from scratch every single turn with zero memory: a unit several tiles into a multi-turn walk toward (say) a water target would get yanked onto a newly-appeared higher-priority-tier task the instant one appeared anywhere on the board, abandoning the walk and starting over -- a real thrashing pattern, not just "greedy nearest-tile" as originally described. Fixed by adding a `unit_targets` dict (`{{unit_idx: (task_key, target)}}`) threaded through `_plan_units` and persisted in each caller's existing per-episode `state` closure (`robust_agent.make_agent`, `train_rl.py`'s `make_training_agent`, and the generated `submission/main.py` template in `build_submission.py`, which needed its own `state` dict added since it didn't have one at all): once a unit picks a target it's not yet standing on, that commitment survives to the next turn and is only abandoned if the target itself becomes invalid (task completed, tile state changed), not because something else became nominally higher-priority. `WATER` deliberately was *not* demoted below `PLANT` despite the movement problem -- confirmed via `docs/GAME_GUIDE.md` that missing even one day of watering turns a plant into a weed with zero grace period, versus HARVEST's gradual decay -- so the fix targets movement waste specifically, not priority reordering, which could have made things worse. Also caught and fixed a second latent instance of the exact bug class from the entry below: the two new module-level dicts `_plan_units` now depends on (`STATIC_TASK_ACTIONS`, `TASK_INFO_KEY`) aren't `def`s, so `build_submission.py`'s AST-based function extractor couldn't see them; added a parallel `_extract_assigns` extractor and a `ROBUST_AGENT_CONSTANTS` list so this class of drift gets caught structurally instead of by accident. 69.3% is still high -- more gains likely available (e.g. genuine multi-task local routing instead of one-task-at-a-time commitment) but out of scope for this pass.
